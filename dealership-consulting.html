Dealership consulting

Dealership performance consulting.

Lewis Lab helps dealership owners and groups improve performance across sales, inventory, marketing, and CRM. We connect market opportunity with the decisions and processes inside the store, then define the priorities, ownership, and measures needed to execute.

The initial engagement

30 days.

A diagnostic scoped to your business priorities and the stores involved. A review of performance, operating practices, and available records, followed by recommendations and a working action plan.

For dealership owners and group operators seeking an independent view of performance, a coordinated growth strategy, and support putting improvements into practice.

Areas of focus

Performance across the dealership.

Sales, inventory, marketing, and operating practices affect each other. The review connects these areas around your business priorities, with the stores, depth, and scope agreed before work begins.

Sales performance

Compare sales pace, market share, model mix, and local demand. Assess whether available inventory, market coverage, and sales execution support the dealership's growth objectives.

Inventory management

Review aging, stocking, pricing, and merchandising by model and vehicle. Set priorities for aged units and align the inventory plan with local sales opportunity.

Marketing effectiveness

Examine audiences, offers, vehicle priorities, spend, and vendor reporting against inventory and sales results. Establish what to continue, adjust, or challenge in the marketing plan.

CRM and customer development

Evaluate lead handling, unsold opportunities, customer records, and follow-up activity. Check whether prospecting routines, assignments, and response quality support the sales priorities.

Process and accountability

Review how sales, marketing, and inventory decisions become day-to-day work. Identify where handoffs, responsibilities, or management review leave the plan incomplete.

Store and market planning

Compare rooftops in the context of their markets, inventory, and operating practices. Develop store-specific priorities and identify practices worth applying across the group.

The approach

From assessment to a working plan.

We agree on the business areas, stores, and depth of review before starting. The initial 30-day diagnostic establishes the findings and priorities that guide strategy and any further implementation work.

  1. Examine

    Review the relevant sales, inventory, market, marketing, and CRM records alongside the way the dealership operates. Establish a baseline and compare reported activity with commercial results.

  2. Isolate

    Identify where market opportunity, inventory, marketing, and execution are misaligned. Distinguish process problems from market conditions or data gaps, and assess which issues have the greatest business impact.

  3. Apply

    Develop recommendations with leadership, set priorities, and define ownership and measures. Scope further support for strategy, implementation, and progress reviews around what the business needs.

What you receive

Findings, priorities, and an action plan.

The diagnostic concludes with a written assessment and a leadership review. The recommendations connect the findings to decisions and actions your team can take forward.

Performance assessment
What the market, sales, inventory, and operating records show within the agreed scope. Findings distinguish supported conclusions from data gaps and issues requiring further review.
Prioritized recommendations
Specific changes to process, stocking, pricing, marketing, CRM activity, or management review, ranked by business impact and your team's capacity to act.
Ownership and measures
An action plan with proposed owners, timing, and baseline measures matched to the objectives, such as sales pace, aged-inventory share, market share, or CRM conversion.

Common questions

Before we start.

What data do you need?

Relevant records may include sales and market reports, inventory and aging data, pricing, marketing plans and vendor reports, and CRM activity. The scope determines what is needed, and we agree on access before starting.

Is this the same as the free Clarity Diagnostic?

The free Clarity Diagnostic is a short self-assessment based on your answers. This is a paid consulting engagement: a structured 30-day review of actual business data and processes, followed by a prioritized action plan.

How does this work with our existing team and vendors?

Lewis Lab works with dealership leadership to assess performance across functions and develop a coordinated plan. Your internal team and vendors can use that plan to align priorities, responsibilities, and measures. Further implementation support is scoped around the work required.

What happens after the 30-day diagnostic?

We review the findings and next actions with leadership. Your team can take the plan forward, or additional execution support can be scoped around the priorities identified.
